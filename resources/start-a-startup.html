How to start a startup
in 2026.

In the age of AI you don't need an engineer, you don't need employees, and you don't need funding. You can build it completely bootstrapped, and this is the best time there has ever been to start. Here is the playbook I follow, step by step.

Why now

What you don't need anymore.

The three things that used to stop people from starting are not the blockers they were. AI does the work that used to need a team, so a founder can start lean and stay in control.

  1. 01

    You don't need an engineer.

    AI coding tools write, test and ship most of the code a first product needs. You still need to understand what you're building and why, but you no longer need to hire someone before you can put a working product in front of customers.

  2. 02

    You don't need employees.

    Support, outreach, scheduling, content drafts and reporting can run through an AI that is connected to your tools. Hire when real demand forces you to, not to get started.

  3. 03

    You don't need funding.

    When the team and the tools cost less, revenue from your first customers can pay for growth. Bootstrapping keeps you focused on what people will actually pay for.

Step by step

The three steps.

Do them in order. Each one feeds the next: an AI-native setup gives you time to create content, content brings customers, and customers tell you what to build.

Step 1 Make your company AI-native

  1. Connect your AI to every platform you use. Your social media, your inbox, your email outreach, your calendar and your documents. An AI that can see your business can actually run parts of it.
  2. It's cheaper. The busywork that would need a hire gets drafted for you, and you only review and approve.
  3. It's faster. Replies, follow-ups and reports go out in minutes instead of days.
  4. It gives you a clearer view of your business. Ask one question and get an answer across your email, calendar and files instead of digging through each one.
  5. My pick is Claude. My personal AI recommendation has always been Claude. Turn on its connectors for the tools you use (for example Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive), then start with the prompts below.

Step 2 Pick a niche and create content

  1. Pick one niche. One kind of customer with one kind of problem. Narrow beats broad when nobody knows you yet.
  2. Start creating content. Post consistently about the problem you solve. You'll find your way as you go.
  3. Find what works and what doesn't. Keep the formats and topics people watch and share, and drop the rest.
  4. Turn views into paid customers. Views compound, and the right views convert into people who pay you. Always give them a clear next step.

Step 3 Get customer feedback

  1. Get as much customer feedback as possible. Every message, call and complaint is data.
  2. Talk to them. Ask what they were trying to do, where they got stuck, and what they'd pay for.
  3. Find their real problems. Listen for the problem behind the feature request.
  4. Solve them as soon as possible. Fix it, tell the customer it's fixed, and you've earned a fan.

Copy these

Prompts to get started with Claude.

Paste these into Claude after you turn on the connectors you need. Claude only drafts here: you stay the one who hits send.

  • Daily inbox brief

    Needs the Gmail connector. Run it every morning.

    Go through my Gmail from the last 24 hours. List every email that needs a reply from me today, most urgent first, and flag anything about payments, customers or deadlines. Then draft replies for the top five in my tone. Don't send anything; leave them as drafts for me to review.

  • Email outreach drafts

    Needs the Gmail connector. Fill in the brackets first.

    Here is what I sell: [one-line offer]. Here is who it's for: [your niche]. Here are my leads: [names, companies, one detail about each]. Write a short, personal first email for each lead: one line about them, one line about the problem I solve, and one easy question to reply to. No buzzwords, under 90 words each. Save them as Gmail drafts and don't send them.

  • Weekly view of the business

    Needs the Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive connectors. Run it every Monday.

    Give me a one-page view of my business for last week using my email, calendar and Drive: what customers asked for, deals or payments that came in or stalled, meetings that need a follow-up, and the three things I should focus on this week. Keep it short and point me to the source email or doc for each item.

  • Customer feedback into fixes

    Paste in feedback, or point it at your support inbox.

    Here is my recent customer feedback: [paste messages, reviews or call notes]. Group it into the underlying problems, rank them by how many customers mention each one, and for the top three suggest the smallest fix I could ship this week. Also write a short reply I can send to the customers who raised each one once it's fixed.

Don't forget

The basics.

Nothing should stop someone from paying you.

  • Do your research. Know your market, your competitors and the customer you're building for before you go all in.
  • Make sure your website works. Fast, clear, works on a phone, and every button goes somewhere.
  • Make sure your payment gateway runs smoothly. Use a proven one like Stripe and test the checkout end to end, on a phone, before you launch.
  • Be good at what you're doing. AI makes you faster. It doesn't replace being genuinely good at the thing you sell.
