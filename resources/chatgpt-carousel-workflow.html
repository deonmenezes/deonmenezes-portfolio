ChatGPT Instagram carousel workflow

A copy-ready workflow for turning one useful idea into a focused seven-slide Instagram carousel with a strong hook, real substance, and a clear call to action.

Plan the story before styling it, write one job per slide, and generate a visual brief that stays consistent across the whole carousel.

The prompts

  1. 01 · Plan the seven-slide story

    Start with one narrow topic and a clearly defined reader.

    Act as an experienced Instagram carousel editor. Plan a seven-slide educational carousel about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]. The reader's current problem is [PROBLEM], and the useful result I want to give them is [RESULT].

Create:
1. A specific, curiosity-driven cover hook without clickbait
2. A slide-by-slide story arc with exactly one job per slide
3. One concrete example, proof point, or demonstration
4. A final slide with a natural call to action: [CTA]

Keep each slide skimmable on a phone. Flag any factual claim that needs a source. Do not write final slide copy yet; first show the structure and explain why each slide earns the next swipe.

  2. 02 · Write concise slide copy

    Use after approving the story arc.

    Write the final copy for the approved seven-slide Instagram carousel.

For every slide provide:
- A short heading
- No more than 35 words of body copy
- One optional visual cue

Use plain, natural language. Vary sentence length, remove filler, and avoid generic hype. Preserve any source notes beside factual claims so I can verify them before publishing. End with the approved call to action, not a new sales pitch.

  3. 03 · Create one visual system

    Finish with this before opening your design tool.

    Turn the approved carousel into a consistent visual brief.

Define one layout system for all seven slides: typography hierarchy, safe margins, grid, color palette, image treatment, icon style, and progress indicator. Make the cover visually distinct while keeping the remaining slides recognizably part of the same set.

For each slide, describe the composition and the single most important visual element. Prioritize readability at phone size, strong contrast, and accessible text. Do not generate fake charts, fake screenshots, logos I do not own, or visual evidence that was not supplied.
