01Start here

The master carousel prompt

This gives every slide a job, controls the word count, and separates writing from design so you can approve the story first.

Act as an expert Instagram carousel strategist, teacher, and editor.

Create a 10-slide Instagram carousel about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE].

Goal: [WHAT THE READER SHOULD KNOW, FEEL, OR DO AFTER SLIDE 10]
Voice: [CLEAR / WITTY / DIRECT / WARM / TECHNICAL]
Reader knowledge: [BEGINNER / INTERMEDIATE / ADVANCED]
Call to action: [SAVE / SHARE / COMMENT / VISIT A LINK / TRY A STEP]

Use this story structure:
1. A specific hook that creates curiosity — 8 words maximum
2. Why this matters to the reader now
3. The common mistake or misconception
4–7. The core lesson, one idea per slide
8. A concrete example, comparison, or mini case study
9. A concise recap or checklist
10. One clear call to action

For every slide, provide:
- Slide number and purpose
- Headline (8 words maximum)
- Body copy (25–45 words maximum)
- One suggested visual or layout
- Useful alt text for accessibility

Make the slides skimmable, specific, and non-repetitive. Use plain language. Do not invent statistics, quotes, sources, or product claims. Mark any factual claim that needs current research with [VERIFY].

First show me only the 10-slide outline. Wait for my approval before writing the final slide copy.
02Worked example

The money carousel from the Reel

This recreates the educational example shown in the video. It is specific enough to produce a useful first draft.

Create a 10-slide Instagram carousel titled “Things Schools Never Teach You About Money.”

Audience: young adults who are new to personal finance.
Goal: explain the basics clearly and give the reader one practical next step.

Cover:
- Emergency funds
- Compound interest
- Inflation
- Budgeting
- Investing basics
- Credit scores
- Good debt versus bad debt
- Taxes and insurance
- A final takeaway

Keep it beginner-friendly, practical, and visually skimmable. Give every slide:
1. A short headline
2. Two to four concise teaching points
3. One real-world example or action
4. One simple visual direction

Avoid financial promises, personalized advice, and unverified numbers. Label anything that needs a current source with [VERIFY]. End with a save-and-share call to action.
03Before design

The ruthless editor prompt

Run this after the draft. It catches a weak hook, too much text, repeated ideas, and an empty final call to action.

Review the carousel draft above as an impatient Instagram reader.

Score it from 1–10 for:
- Hook strength
- Clarity
- Swipe momentum
- Specificity
- Usefulness
- Credibility
- Strength of the final call to action

Then identify:
1. The first slide where attention is likely to drop
2. Repeated, generic, or unnecessary sentences
3. Claims that need fact-checking or a source
4. Slides trying to communicate more than one main idea
5. Headlines that are too long or vague

Rewrite only the weak slides. Keep each headline under 8 words and each body under 45 words. Preserve my voice. Do not add hype, fake urgency, or invented facts.

Return the polished 10-slide copy in one clean table.
04Canva handoff

The visual system prompt

This turns the approved copy into one coherent design brief instead of ten unrelated slide styles.

Turn the approved carousel copy into a practical Canva design brief.

Brand:
- Primary color: [HEX]
- Accent color: [HEX]
- Background: [HEX]
- Heading style: [SERIF / SANS / EDITORIAL / BOLD]
- Body style: [CLEAN / HUMAN / TECHNICAL]
- Visual mood: [MINIMAL / PLAYFUL / PREMIUM / FUTURISTIC]

Create one reusable visual system for all 10 slides. Include:
1. A cover layout
2. Two repeatable content layouts
3. One example or comparison layout
4. A recap layout
5. A final CTA layout
6. Type hierarchy with approximate sizes
7. Spacing, margins, and safe-area guidance for 1080 × 1350 px
8. Guidance for icons, photos, diagrams, and slide numbers

Keep body text readable on a phone. Use high color contrast. Do not put important text near the edges. Do not redesign the copy; flag any overflow instead.

Finish with a slide-by-slide layout assignment I can follow in Canva.

A cleaner workflow

Script first.
Design second.

01

Plan in ChatGPT

Paste the master prompt, replace the brackets, and approve the outline before touching fonts or colors.

02

Pressure-test it

Use the editor prompt to remove repetition, sharpen the opening, and make every swipe earn the next one.

03

Build in Canva

Paste the approved copy into one reusable template. Keep type, spacing, and visual rhythm consistent.

The rule that saves the most time:

Do not ask ChatGPT to rewrite the copy while you are fixing the design. Freeze the words first, then build the slides.
