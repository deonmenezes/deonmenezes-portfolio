The full list of low-calorie, high-satiety foods

Foods that fill you up for few calories, grouped so you can shop from it. Each one shows roughly how many calories and how much protein are in 100 g, from USDA FoodData Central.

Why they fill you up

A full plate for few calories.

These foods are mostly water and fibre, or high in protein, so a big portion costs few calories. In the Satiety Index study (Holt et al., 1995), people ate 240 kcal of 38 common foods; the more protein, fibre and water a food had, the fuller it kept them. Boiled potatoes scored highest, at 323% of white bread.

  • WaterCucumber, lettuce and watermelon are over 90% water, so they fill your stomach.
  • FibreVegetables, fruit and dal add bulk, and fibre went with feeling fuller in the study.
  • ProteinEgg whites, chicken, fish, dahi and dal kept people fuller.
  • Volume100 g of cauliflower rice is 25 kcal. 100 g of cooked white rice is 130.

Keep in mind

Read this once.

  • The numbers are approximate.They are USDA averages per 100 g, raw unless the food says cooked or boiled. Your brand or recipe can differ, so check labels.
  • Cooking changes everything.Oil, ghee, butter, sugar and sauces add calories fast. The same vegetables fried can be a different meal.
  • A satiety score is not a calorie count.The Satiety Index compared foods at the same 240 kcal. Only the foods that study tested have a score, so this page gives none for the rest.
  • General information, not medical advice.If you have a health condition, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian first.
