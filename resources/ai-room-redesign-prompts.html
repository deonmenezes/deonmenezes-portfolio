AI Room Redesign Prompt Pack

Five copy-ready ChatGPT prompts that turn a room photo into realistic concepts, a dimensioned plan, and a budget-aware shopping list.

Go from “what should I change?” to a room you can actually build—while preserving the architecture, openings, and real dimensions already in your space.

Use this safely

A practical starting checklist

  1. 01

    Photograph the room from two opposite corners in daylight, then note the wall lengths, ceiling height, door and window sizes, and anything that cannot move.

  2. 02

    Run the prompts in order. Answer the clarification questions before asking for images so the concepts respect how you really use the room.

  3. 03

    Choose one concept, verify every measurement yourself, and use the final plan and shopping list as a brief—not as construction drawings.

The prompts

  1. 01 · Analyze the room before designing

    Start here after uploading 2–4 photos and your measurements.

    Act as a professional interior designer and space planner. Analyze the room photos and measurements I uploaded before proposing any design.

Room purpose: [WHAT I USE THE ROOM FOR]
People using it: [WHO / HOW MANY]
Location and climate: [CITY / COUNTRY]
Must keep: [FURNITURE, ART, STORAGE, ETC.]
Cannot move: [WINDOWS, DOORS, RADIATORS, PLUMBING, BUILT-INS]
Main problems: [CLUTTER, LIGHT, LAYOUT, STORAGE, ETC.]
Style direction: warm modern, calm, functional, and realistic.

Preserve the existing architecture, wall positions, ceiling height, windows, doors, and room dimensions. Do not invent hidden measurements or structural changes.

First, give me:
1. Observed facts from the photos
2. Assumptions you need me to verify
3. Layout, lighting, storage, and circulation problems
4. The room’s strongest existing features
5. Up to five high-impact clarification questions

Do not generate a redesign yet. Wait for my answers.

  2. 02 · Generate three distinct concepts

    Use after answering the room-analysis questions.

    Using the verified room analysis above, create three distinct warm-modern interior concepts for this exact room.

Keep the existing architecture, dimensions, camera viewpoint, windows, doors, and all non-movable elements unchanged. Every concept must be functional, realistic, and possible to implement in the real room.

For Concept A, B, and C, include:
- A memorable concept name and one-sentence idea
- Furniture layout and circulation logic
- Color palette with wall, wood, textile, and accent colors
- Materials, lighting layers, storage, and styling
- What stays, what moves, and what gets replaced
- The main benefit and tradeoff
- A realistic implementation difficulty: easy, moderate, or involved

Make the three directions meaningfully different—not the same room with different cushions. Then generate one photorealistic image for each concept, clearly labeled A, B, and C. If image generation would require changing the room geometry, stop and explain the conflict instead.

  3. 03 · Refine the selected render

    Paste this after choosing Concept A, B, or C.

    Refine Concept [A / B / C] using the original room photo as the visual base.

Keep the exact room geometry, camera angle, ceiling, floor footprint, windows, doors, and fixed architectural details. Do not widen the room, raise the ceiling, add openings, hide doors, or place furniture where it blocks circulation.

Apply these refinements:
- Keep: [ITEMS / FEATURES]
- Change: [COLORS / FURNITURE / LIGHTING]
- Add: [STORAGE / ART / PLANTS / TEXTILES]
- Remove: [ITEMS]
- Mood: warm modern, inviting, uncluttered, and lived-in
- Practical needs: [KIDS / PETS / RENTAL / WORK-FROM-HOME / ACCESSIBILITY]

Create a photorealistic revised image with believable proportions, natural material texture, and realistic daylight. After the image, list every visible change you made and flag anything whose fit still depends on a measurement.

  4. 04 · Turn the concept into an implementation plan

    Use only after you have supplied measurements and approved a concept.

    Turn the approved room concept into a practical implementation plan using my verified measurements.

Clearly separate:
- Measurements I provided
- Your recommended dimensions
- Any measurement that is still missing

Include:
1. A wall-by-wall layout with furniture placement and walking clearances
2. Maximum furniture dimensions that will fit each position
3. Paint direction with finish, undertone, and sample color references; do not invent a manufacturer code
4. Lighting plan with fixture type, position, color temperature, brightness range, and control strategy
5. Window treatments, rug size, art scale, storage, and cable management
6. A step-by-step order of work from clearing the room to final styling
7. A pre-purchase verification checklist

Use centimeters and inches. Never claim the layout is precisely scaled unless all required measurements are present. Flag electrical, structural, or installation work that needs a qualified professional.

  5. 05 · Build a prioritized shopping list

    Finish with this prompt once the implementation plan is settled.

    Create a budget-aware shopping list for the approved room plan.

Location: [CITY / COUNTRY]
Currency: [CURRENCY]
Total budget: [AMOUNT]
Items I already own: [LIST]
Quality priorities: [WHERE TO INVEST]
Deadline: [DATE]

Organize the list into:
A. Essentials that make the room function
B. High-impact improvements
C. Nice-to-have finishing touches

For every item include quantity, target dimensions, material or finish, color, realistic price range, useful search terms, and one lower-cost alternative. Add estimated subtotals, delivery or installation allowances, and a 10–15% contingency.

Do not invent live prices, stock, discounts, or product links. If current products are needed, ask permission to search the web and verify each item. End with the best purchase order so I do not buy decor before solving layout, lighting, and storage.

Keep in mind

AI images can change scale, windows, doors, or circulation without warning. Check the proposal against your measurements and consult a qualified professional before structural, electrical, plumbing, or load-bearing work.
